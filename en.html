1 September 2026
  • KISA and MSIT will pilot an AI penetration-testing agent that designs intrusion scenarios based on target companies' website structures, as part of the second-half cyber crisis response drill running October 19-30. [1]
  • The Broadcasting and Media Communications Commission's 2027 budget allocates about 34 billion won to its disinformation response transparency center and fact-checking system, alongside new funding for broadcast AI training data. [2]
  • A capital markets research report finds that Korean financial firms' generative AI use is expanding into investment advice, credit assessment, and customer consulting after network-separation deregulation, and calls for tracking accumulated risk experience to inform follow-up rules. [3]
August 2026 (24 days)
31 August 2026
  • KISA and KCA held an AI Privacy Challenge mock-hacking competition where college students used generative AI to uncover vulnerabilities in personal-data processing systems, while institutional security staff practiced real-time threat detection and incident recovery. [1]
  • Hong Jung-woo took office as standing vice-chair of the National AI Strategy Committee, pledging to coordinate AI governance across government and industry and emphasizing the need for sovereign AI systems in defense and public-sector use. [2]
30 August 2026
  • Korea's presidential office restructured its AI policy leadership, naming Lee Hae-min as AI future planning secretary, reappointing Ha Jung-woo as full-time vice chair of the National AI Strategy Committee, and confirming Bae Kyung-hoon as science minister overseeing the Independent AI Foundation Model project and national AI computing center. [1][2][3][4]
29 August 2026
  • MSIT selected SK Telecom, KT, and Kakao as operators for the 'Everyone's AI' free public AI service, with each firm developing GPU-supported search, booking, and payment features to launch later this year. [1]
28 August 2026
  • MSIT's Independent AI Foundation Model (Dokpamo) project drew a transparency dispute after Motif Technologies, despite ranking first on benchmarks, was eliminated in round-two review for weaker expert/user scores; it demanded disclosure of detailed scoring and the ministry agreed to review the appeal within 15 days. [1][2][3][4]
  • MSIT, NIA, TTA, and ETRI jointly issued guidelines on determining high-impact AI, provider duties for high-impact AI, and AI transparency and safety assurance under Korea's AI Act. [5]
  • MSIT selected the 'Everyone's AI' operators, choosing SK Telecom, Kakao, and KT consortiums to build a free national AI chatbot, backed by 512 Nvidia B200 GPUs and government funding from 2027. [6][7][8][9]
27 August 2026
  • Korea's AI service user-protection public-private council held its second meeting to review illegal/harmful content risks and regulatory gaps, with Kakao presenting its Kanana Safeguard guardrail model for safety verification. [1][2]
  • The International AI Ethics Association urged Korea's National Education Commission to fully reconsider using AI for first-pass grading of essay-type exam answers, citing bias, lack of explainability, hallucination risk, and inadequate remedy procedures. [3]
  • MSIT and NIA released 29 AI training datasets (about 35.44 million items, ~1.56 trillion tokens) built under the Independent AI Foundation Model project by five selected teams, after quality and safety verification, for free public use via AI Hub. [4][5]
26 August 2026
  • Korea's Independent AI Foundation Model project advanced three teams — SK Telecom, LG AI Research, and Upstage — past its second evaluation round based on benchmark, expert, and user scoring, while Motif Technologies was eliminated. [1]
  • ETRI joined the Agentic AI Foundation under the Linux Foundation to help shape agentic AI standards such as MCP, working alongside roughly 250 member companies on accuracy, security, and governance workstreams. [2]
  • Korea's National Assembly Research Service flagged implementation gaps in the newly effective AI and Data-Based Administration Act, citing unresolved issues in data quality, AI reliability/accountability, and governance/security ahead of its August 28 enforcement date. [3]
25 August 2026
  • AhnLab warned of a new attack called “memory poisoning” that injects false data or malicious rules into an AI agent's long-term memory to persistently distort its judgment, and Korea's data protection authority plans AI agent guidelines later this year. [1][2]
  • The government finalized Korea's AI Ethics Principles under the AI Basic Act, built on three values and seven principles (human-centricity, privacy, fairness, accountability, safety, reliability, transparency), issued as a non-binding self-regulatory code with plans for explainers and self-check tools. [3][4][5][6]
  • ETRI joined the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation (AAIF), a roughly 250-member group including Google and OpenAI, to help shape international standards such as MCP for agentic AI. [7]
24 August 2026
  • MSIT finalized Korea's new AI ethics principles, built on three values and seven principles, explicitly designating users, government, and civil society as "AI actors" alongside suppliers, and requiring human control over AI agents plus stronger accountability for large AI providers. [1][2][3][4][5][6][7]
  • Commentary flagged that because the revised principles remain a non-binding self-regulatory code, expanded civic oversight and eventual legislation are needed to make them effective against harms like job loss and privacy violations. [7][1][2][3][4][5]
22 August 2026
  • Korea amended its Personal Information Protection Act to allow use of personal data for AI development when pseudonymized or anonymized data is insufficient, subject to review by the Personal Information Protection Commission and mandatory prior risk assessment for sensitive data; the change takes effect 6 months after promulgation. [1]
21 August 2026
  • South Korea's National Assembly passed an amendment to the Personal Information Protection Act allowing lawfully collected personal data—including sensitive or unique-identifier data—to be used for AI development under enhanced safety measures and Personal Information Protection Commission review, with mandatory prior risk assessment and public disclosure; it takes effect six months after promulgation. [1]
20 August 2026
  • South Korea's National Assembly passed an amendment to the Personal Information Protection Act allowing original personal data to be used for AI training when public interest is recognized, subject to review by the Personal Information Protection Commission, mandatory prior risk assessments for high-risk data processing, and public disclosure requirements. [1]
19 August 2026
  • MSIT's second-round evaluation of the Independent AI Foundation Model project (Dokpamo) passed Upstage, SK Telecom, and LG AI Research to the next stage while eliminating Motif Technologies, based on benchmark, expert, and user-evaluation scores. [1][2][3][4][5]
  • Critics noted the government did not disclose team-by-team scores or rankings from the tax-funded Dokpamo evaluation, despite using a 40/35/25-point benchmark-expert-user rubric. [6]
  • MSIT is reviewing a broader restructuring of the Dokpamo and related AI programs, including future competition rules, resource allocation, and whether a fourth selection stage will proceed. [1]
18 August 2026
  • South Korea's Independent AI Foundation Model (Dokpamo) project eliminated Motif Technologies in its second-stage evaluation despite top benchmark scores, advancing Upstage, SK Telecom, and LG AI Research to the third round; the government said a review with global evaluator Artificial Analysis found no evidence of benchmark-targeted overfitting. [1][2][3]
  • The Korea Institute of Finance called for financial firms to establish AI governance frameworks covering fairness, transparency, consumer protection, and data security, urging regulators to set minimum explainability standards for black-box models while letting firms implement governance autonomously via sandboxes. [4]
17 August 2026
  • MSIT's revised second draft of AI ethics principles reportedly removed or weakened anti-discrimination provisions covering gender, age, and disability, as well as developer/provider accountability language present in the first draft. The Korean Women's Associations United criticized the draft for shifting bias/discrimination responsibility onto users and demanded explicit non-discrimination and inclusion principles with clear duties for each actor across the AI lifecycle. [1]
14 August 2026
  • MSIT and KISDI unveiled a draft revision of Korea's AI ethics principles, updating the 2020 standards into 7 principles (including safety, reliability, human dignity, privacy, and fairness) built on 3 core values, designed as non-binding soft-law guidance rather than new regulation. [1][2]
13 August 2026
  • South Korea's government proposed an amendment to the Personal Information Protection Act that would expand permissible use of personal data for AI development when enhanced safeguards are in place, subject to review by the Personal Information Protection Commission; the bill is currently under National Assembly deliberation after passing preliminary readings in May and July 2026. [1]
12 August 2026
  • The Board of Audit and Inspection found that public institutions have built duplicate and poor-quality AI training datasets despite over 1.6 trillion won in government investment, and recommended MSIT introduce prior data-sharing checks and unified quality standards. [1]
11 August 2026
  • KISA and IITP convened an industry council to shape information-security R&D priorities, focusing on AI agent security, zero-trust, and physical-AI risks. [1]
  • The Korea Institute of Finance issued a brief urging regulators to prepare consumer-protection rules for AI shopping agents before they reach retail financial customers, citing disclosure and conflict-of-interest risks. [2]
9 August 2026
  • MSIT announced an 'Agentic AI Initiative' covering physical AI safety, including plans for safety guidelines and sandbox support for humanoid robots after a forum raised concerns about malfunction risks from physical manipulation. [1]
7 August 2026
  • Korea lacks pre-release safety verification for AI models: MSIT's AI Safety Institute evaluated 42 domestic and foreign AI models but found no cases of pre-launch review, unlike the US system where firms submit models to government for pre-release assessment. [1]
6 August 2026
  • MSIT and KISA published an AI security red-teaming guide to help companies conduct pre-emptive risk checks on training data, core models, applications, and autonomous agent behavior. [1]
4 August 2026
  • MSIT released AI data quality management guides, including a generative-AI-specific data quality guide and an AI data construction guide. [1]
  • The Ministry of Health and Welfare issued guidelines on the use of health and medical data. [2]
  • The Personal Information Protection Commission published guidelines on the processing of pseudonymized information. [3]
3 August 2026
  • Korea's Personal Information Protection Commission urged Samsung Electronics and Meta to build in privacy safeguards for upcoming AI glasses, such as recording-indicator alerts to protect bystanders, and plans to establish related privacy principles this year. [1]
2 August 2026
  • AI-Ethics-KR criticized Korea's autonomous vehicle law and the proposed physical AI special act for including vague "unavoidable" personal-data-use exemptions that prioritize industry competitiveness over clear standards or civil-society input. [1]
