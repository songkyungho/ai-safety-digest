8 October 2026
  • SK Telecom and Upstage launched a cyber-security AI consortium with six security firms and three universities, training A.X K and Solar on malware, vulnerability and security-monitoring data. The consortium will use mutual red-teaming between the two models, with Korea University, Soongsil University and Pusan National University designing and evaluating as third parties. [1]
  • The Ministry of Health and Welfare and the Korean Neuropsychiatric Association issued guidelines on using generative AI for mental health, covering overreliance, privacy checks and not mistaking AI advice for medical diagnosis. A separate section gives 7 recommendations for minors and guardians, and the ministry plans tailored guidance for high-risk groups in 2027. [2]
  • KISA is recruiting teams for the 2026 AI hacking defense contest (ACDC), covering AI for Security, Security for AI and AI Platform Security. Qualifiers are held online on Oct 31, and 40 teams advance to the Seoul finals on Dec 1. [3]
7 October 2026
  • President Lee Jae-myung ordered a shift from after-the-fact response to preventive security after repeated financial and public-sector data leaks, some with signs of AI-assisted hacking. Regulators shared 28 unique attacker IPs, and at Shinhan Bank, where about 25,000 customers' data was exposed, wording tied to the AI penetration-testing tool ARTEX was reportedly found on a suspected attack web server.[1][2]
  • MSIT Minister Bae Kyung-hoon said the ministry will publish a national AI safety ecosystem masterplan in December. He agreed that safety and trust funding of 27.4 billion won, against 6.149 trillion won for AI performance infrastructure, is too small, and said human control mechanisms such as a kill switch are needed.[3]
6 October 2026
  • Seven Korean financial firms were hit by a suspected AI-driven intrusion tool, ARTEX, with about 67,000 records leaked. PM Han Sung-sook ordered neglected government websites shut down, a review of security governance, and a re-check of privacy certification, and police opened a formal investigation. [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8][9]
  • MSIT's AI Safety Institute ran prompt-injection tests on autonomous AI agents. No LLM was found that blocks attacks completely, with average defense rates of 53.3–93.9% by model, and every tested model failed against malicious commands planted in stored information. [10]
  • At the National Assembly audit, a lawmaker noted that only 3 of 538 approved AI medical devices were submitted to MSIT for a high-impact AI check. The AI Basic Act does not require firms to submit or register their self-assessments, and all 3 reviewed devices were ruled not high-impact, including a brain-hemorrhage reader. MSIT said it would review reflecting the issue in system improvements. [11][12]
5 October 2026
  • AI-assisted hacking spread from major banks to savings banks and capital firms, with personal data leaked from seven financial firms, about 70,000 records. Police are investigating traces of the open-source autonomous pentest tool ARTEX AI. Meanwhile KISA's new AI cyber-threat vulnerability management center saw its requested 20 extra staff and KRW 17 billion project go unfunded in the government budget proposal, and the National Assembly science committee audit on Oct 6 is expected to take this up. [1][2][3]
  • The Youth Counseling & Welfare Institute gave private firms 14,282 pseudonymized youth crisis-counseling records (suicide, self-harm, sexual violence) for AI training on July 29, with no notice to users and no separate consent. Re-identification risk has been raised because conversation text was included and only identifiers were changed. [4]
  • Of 31 models on the government-wide AI common platform, 19 are foreign-made, including BAAI's Bge-M3, which is on the US Entity List. The government admitted it had no vetting criteria for a model's country or developer of origin. [5]
4 October 2026
  • After signs that AI hacking tools were used in a data leak at a Korean financial firm, security experts called for defensive AI systems and a deputy-PM-level cross-government cybersecurity control tower. Cyber functions are currently split across the presidential national security office, MSIT, the National Intelligence Service and the Personal Information Protection Commission, and the experts also urged executive accountability, including CEOs, for breaches. [1]
2 October 2026
  • MSIT's budget submission to the National Assembly merges the Independent AI Foundation Model project (Dokpamo) into a frontier-AI data program. Funding shifts from a 25 billion won grant this year to a 750 billion won equity investment via the Korea Development Bank next year (total 800 billion won, including 50 billion won for cybersecurity AI training infrastructure). Lawmakers' offices say investment-selection criteria, the government's stake and recovery terms, and budget details remain unclear.[1][2]
  • The Ministry of the Interior and Safety finalized public-sector AI ethics standards on Oct 1, built on 6 core values (publicness, equity, transparency, accountability, safety, privacy) with 68 checklist items and a three-tier self-assessment system. The accountability standard states that humans remain ultimately responsible even when AI supports or makes administrative decisions, and it requires human oversight and remedy mechanisms.[3]
  • At the 5th Chief AI Officer (CAIO) council, the National AI Strategy Committee presented a draft AI legislative framework centered on the AI Basic Act. It covers closing legal gaps in defense and security, providing predictability for businesses, and responding immediately to AI harms; attendance expanded to 42 central agencies. The Personal Information Protection Commission pushed for stronger agency-level privacy management after recent hacking and leak incidents, and ministries were asked to help set standards for AI agents' use of personal data.[4][5]
1 October 2026
  • The National AI Strategy Committee held a seminar on calls to slow AI development and promoted a "speed responsibility" approach: Korea should build frontier capability together with safety, security and control. Experts urged independent model evaluation, advanced-AI risk assessment and safety verification, and international coordination; one speaker said existing rules cannot control frontier AI risks. [1][2][3][4]
  • MOIS and the Personal Information Protection Commission will hold the third "Everyone's Discussion" forum on AI-era personal data protection and use on Nov. 1 with 200 citizens. The government plans to announce its policy direction within the year. [5][6]
  • The Personal Information Protection Commission met pseudonymized-data support centers and Personal Information Innovation Zone operators in Busan to gather field input on AI-era data rules. It plans to pursue institutional changes to fit new data-use methods. [7]
September 2026 (20 days)
30 September 2026
  • MSIT will run a government–private working group from October to draft a national AI safety ecosystem master plan, covering an AI safety management framework, response capacity for advanced high-performance AI risks, and safe everyday AI use. About 25 members include the AI Safety Institute, NIA, KISDI and TTA, and the plan is due by December.[1][2][3][4]
  • The Korea Media and Communications Commission plans AI-generated content labeling duties and platform management duties, including disclosure of how recommendation algorithms work and when they change, and user choice over recommendations. It set out 4 goals and 12 tasks, with legal amendments planned within the year.[5]
  • The Personal Information Protection Commission marked Personal Information Protection Day by pledging to overhaul privacy protection for the AI era and strengthen corporate and institutional accountability and prevention systems.[6]
29 September 2026
  • Eight months into the AI Basic Act, the MSIT-backed support desk logged 461 online inquiries, 212 on transparency duties and 117 on high-impact AI. Experts urged clearer legal or guideline criteria on who must apply watermarks and how, and on what counts as high-impact AI.[1]
  • Korea's Personal Information Protection Commission plans to publish criteria on what information and authority can be entrusted to AI agents within the year. The item appeared in coverage of Meta's Muse agent, which uses a payment path that keeps card details from the agent.[2]
  • The Ministry of the Interior and Safety launched the first statutory AI and data administration officers' council, and plans to distribute AI impact assessment guidelines by December. It also plans to set up a public AI and data association by 2027.[3][4]
28 September 2026
  • The Personal Information Protection Commission hosted a Global AI Privacy Forum in Seoul, bringing together international regulators, GPA leadership, and firms like Microsoft, Meta, and Sony to discuss AI innovation, cross-border cooperation, and child/youth privacy protections. [1][2]
  • MSIT is reportedly reviewing whether to halt the Independent AI Foundation Model (Dokpamo) project and instead create a public-private SPC (30% government stake) to fund a frontier-level model, amid concern the shift could favor large conglomerates over startups; over 2000억원 has already been spent and MSIT says no decision on a third evaluation round or the SPC has been made. [3][4][5]
  • MSIT and IITP launched policy research on regulatory frameworks for 6G/AI network infrastructure, selecting institutions in October for 13 new projects running through 2027년 7월, covering AI-autonomous network design/verification standards, human-intervention criteria, and net-neutrality policy for AI environments. [6]
23 September 2026
  • MSIT and NAVER Cloud began developing a Korean cybersecurity-specialized foundation model, backed by 256 Nvidia B200 GPUs for 10 months, aiming to integrate a 700B-scale model built on ~830TB of security data and shift Korea's information security systems to domestic AI by 2027; KISA separately said a Korea-specific security model is needed since only about half of AI-flagged vulnerabilities prove genuinely valid. [1][2][3]
  • The Personal Information Protection Commission held its AI privacy public-private council to discuss agentic AI risks, with interviews across 15 organizations flagging prompt injection and excessive AI authority as top concerns; it plans to define boundaries for autonomous vs. human-approved tasks, retention/deletion rules for logs and memory, and to release an agentic AI privacy guide by year-end ahead of the amended Personal Information Protection Act taking effect in March 2027. [4][5][6]
  • The Intellectual Property Office co-hosted a strategy forum with the Korean Academy of Engineering on IP rights covering AI models, synthetic data, and workplace know-how, and plans institutional reforms to clarify rights and establish fair compensation systems. [7]
22 September 2026
  • MSIT and KISA piloted GPT-5.5 Cyber scans of national critical-infrastructure websites and software, and experts confirmed about 50–60% of the AI-flagged candidates as valid vulnerabilities. Notification time for companies fell from up to a month to within a week, but KISA did not disclose the total count, the target institutions, or the specific flaws. [1]
  • MSIT discussed development plans and government support with the Naver Cloud consortium, which has begun building a cybersecurity-specialized AI foundation model for critical national facilities and industrial sites. Naver Cloud was selected as the project's implementer on September 3. [2]
21 September 2026
  • MSIT finalized a national R&D AI research ethics guide requiring fact-checking, transparency, and disclosure of AI use, banning submission of evaluation materials to external AI and AI-driven distortion of reviews, with 7 recommended practices for researchers [1].
  • The Korea Economic Research Institute published a report on competition issues in the AI agent era, examining bundling, vertical integration, and self-preferencing risks and proposing policy responses for Korea [2].
  • Prime Minister Han convened the first meeting of the Global AI Hub Promotion Committee, reviewing site-selection plans with a public hearing set for October 2026 ahead of a target 2027 hub opening [3].
20 September 2026
  • MSIT and KISTEP issued an AI research ethics guide for national R&D researchers, requiring disclosure of generative AI use in papers and classifying hidden prompts that manipulate peer review as research misconduct. [1][2]
  • MSIT recommended four major companies' nine labs (LG Electronics, HD Hyundai Robotics, Samsung Medison, Samsung Electronics) for the revived AI alternative military service program, seeking 81 researchers against an annual quota of 120 for 2027-2029. [3][4]
18 September 2026
  • KISA agreed to expand AI-related cybersecurity cooperation with the World Economic Forum's Cybersecurity Center and Singapore's Cyber Security Agency, covering AI security talent, threat information sharing, and critical infrastructure protection. [1]
17 September 2026
  • IITP announced it will fund R&D on AI-based intelligent security and autonomous defense to counter expanding attack surfaces from AI agents and physical AI, covering zero-trust and supply-chain security. [1]
  • Deputy PM Bahk Gyung-hoon said Korea cannot slow its AI development pace but must faster build safety/trust systems for deepfakes, cybersecurity and privacy, amid a debate sparked by rankings showing no Korean model in the global top 25 (best domestic model, Motif Technologies' Motif 3, ranked 31st). [2][3]
  • A commentary argued that before proceeding with the 2500 billion won 'Everyone's AI' project, the government and telecoms must first verify and strengthen zero-trust security, 24-hour monitoring, and breach-response systems given recent telecom data leaks. [4]
16 September 2026
  • KISA is using frontier AI models to scan security products' source code for vulnerabilities, providing analysis reports and remediation guidance, and trained about 50 security staff on AI-based vulnerability response [1].
  • Deputy PM Bae Kyung-hoon rejected US-style AI slowdown calls, saying Korea as a follower nation cannot ease AI development speed, while pledging technical and institutional safeguards for deepfakes, cybersecurity, privacy, and jobs alongside AI Act implementation and the next phase of the Dokpamo project [2][3].
15 September 2026
  • Experts urged Korea to pair its AI investment push with safety infrastructure, noting plans to spend 4.7 trillion won on the Independent AI Foundation Model project and frontier AI next year alongside safety evaluation, risk-behavior detection, and emergency shutdown capabilities. [1]
  • MSIT, the transport ministry, police, and Hyundai Motor/Kia ran a joint public-private cyber-incident drill simulating an AI-driven attack that tampered with vehicle software update files, testing detection, isolation, and threat-sharing procedures. [2]
  • Korea's National Data Agency is hosting an OECD/ADB workshop on AI-friendly global data standards in Seoul, drawing data experts from 25 countries to discuss AI-linked data management and a closing hackathon. [3]
14 September 2026
  • Korea's broadcasting and communications regulator is drafting a dedicated AI User Protection Act that would require AI service providers to disclose information and honor user rights such as access, refusal of use, and explanation requests, alongside a planned dispute mediation body. [1]
10 September 2026

9 September 2026
  • The Financial Supervisory Service held a meeting with chief information security officers (CISOs) from across the financial sector on frontier-AI-enabled cyber threats, warning that AI hacking tools and malware make mass automated attacks more likely. It plans an AI usage guideline, stronger executive accountability, on-site inspections and strict action against weak institutions. [1]
  • The National Assembly Research Service said children and teenagers face risks from AI chatbots, including emotional dependence and overuse that can lead to high-risk behavior. It called for prevention-focused legislation and policy, citing safety-by-design and operator-liability trends abroad. [2]
  • The National AI Strategy Committee marked its first anniversary with a progress report and plans a stronger coordinating role. Its agenda includes improving the evaluation system for the Independent AI Foundation Model project, GPU allocation principles and a K-AI cybersecurity framework. [3][4]
8 September 2026
  • A promulgated amendment to the Personal Information Protection Act adds an AI-development exception allowing personal data that is hard to anonymize or pseudonymize to be used beyond its original purpose. Use requires safeguards and a Personal Information Protection Commission (PIPC) review, with risk assessment and conditions where needed; the law takes effect March 9, 2027. [1]
  • MSIT began a study on safe-use guidelines for AI glasses, covering exam cheating and unauthorized filming. The PIPC has also started drafting personal-data processing principles for AI glasses. [2]
  • The government will split 170 billion won in Everyone's AI GPU support among the SK Telecom, KT and Kakao consortia according to user numbers and service usage. The first evaluation is slated for as early as March 2027, and low scorers will not be dropped. [3]
7 September 2026

  • At a defense AX and security conference, experts called for integrated governance across the Ministry of National Defense, MSIT, and the National Intelligence Service to build AI-based defense systems beyond K-RMF and zero-trust as drones, autonomous weapons, and cyber warfare converge. [2]

2 September 2026

1 September 2026
