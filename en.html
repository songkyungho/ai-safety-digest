3 November 2026
  • The Personal Information Protection Commission (PIPC) and the Korea CPO Council will hold a 2026 Personal Information Policy Forum on AI-driven breach threats on November 9. PIPC will present stronger CEO/CPO accountability and fine reductions tied to proactive security investment, followed by sessions on autonomous AI security systems. [1][2]
  • LG Uplus signed an agreement with Seoul Women's University for joint research on re-identification risk analysis and automatic sensitive-data detection and pseudonymization, centered on the PIPC's Innovation Zone at the university. Results are planned for use in future telecom services. [3]
  • NIA's The LENS report compares US, China and EU AI robotics policy and finds that the US and China develop voluntary robot-specific standards while the EU legislates safety, liability and security requirements. Its policy implications include early participation in international standards. [4]
2 November 2026
  • MSIT and the Korea Media and Communications Commission remain split on extending AI-generated content labeling to the distribution stage. The AI Basic Act currently covers only generative AI developers and service providers, and no draft revision had been submitted by end-October despite a National Assembly subcommittee request for one by early October. [1]
  • A bill by Rep. Park Jung-hoon would ban automated manipulation of search rankings, reviews and view counts and require platforms to prevent it. MSIT urged caution, noting AI mimics input patterns and rotates IPs; a separate bill by Rep. Cho In-chul would add child and youth protection duties for conversational and generative AI providers. [2]
  • Personal data breach reports to the Personal Information Protection Commission hit 579 in January-August, already above the 447 for all of last year. Firms facing the 72-hour reporting rule are crowding law firms and security vendors, which cite AI-lowered attack barriers. [3]
1 November 2026
  • LH obtained NIA's AI ethics compliance mark, which requires an institution to set its own AI ethics principles and have staff complete ethics training. 85.7% of LH's target employees completed the training under guidelines issued in April. [1][2][3]
  • MSIT appointed former Kakao VP Kim Se-woong to head the National AI Service Innovation Task Force. The 61-person unit uses private developers hired as fixed-term civil servants instead of outside contractors. [4]
