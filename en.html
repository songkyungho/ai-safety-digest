29 August 2026
  • The Ministry of Science and ICT selected SK Telecom, KT, and Kakao as operators for the 'Everyone's AI' national public chatbot program, which will provide free AI services for tasks like search, reservations, and payments, backed by government GPU support. [1]
28 August 2026
  • The Independent AI Foundation Model (Dokpamo) second-round evaluation drew formal objections after Motif Technologies, despite ranking first on benchmark scores, was eliminated in favor of SK Telecom, Upstage, and LG AI Research, prompting demands for score disclosure and a re-review; MSIT is reviewing the appeal and the minister is reportedly considering restructuring the program around a single top-tier model instead of an elimination format. [1][2][3][4]
  • MSIT, NIA, TTA, and ETRI jointly issued four new guidelines covering high-impact AI determination, high-impact AI provider duties, AI transparency, and AI safety assurance. [5]
  • MSIT selected the Everyone's AI national chatbot operators — an SK Telecom, Kakao, and KT consortium — from six applicants via written and presentation evaluation, with beta service planned for September–October and government-funded nationwide operation to follow. [6][7][8][9]
27 August 2026
  • The Broadcasting Media Communications Commission held its second public-private council on AI user protection, discussing illegal/harmful content risks and self-regulatory safeguards, with Kakao presenting its Kanana Safeguard guardrail model for Korean-language AI safety verification [1][2].
  • The International AI Ethics Association urged a full review of plans to use AI for first-pass grading of essay-type CSAT exam answers, citing bias, lack of explainability, hallucination risk, and unclear remedy procedures for scoring errors [3].
  • MSIT and NIA released 29 training datasets (about 35.44 million records, ~1.56 trillion tokens) built under the Independent AI Foundation Model project by five selected teams including Naver Cloud, Upstage, SKT, NC AI, and LG AI Research, for use in pretraining, multimodal, and red-teaming work [4][5].
26 August 2026
  • In the second-round evaluation of the Independent AI Foundation Model project (Dokpamo), SK Telecom, LG AI Research, and Upstage advanced while Motif Technologies was eliminated, based on a scoring mix of benchmarks, expert review, and user evaluation. [1]
  • Korea's revised AI and Data-based Administration Act takes effect on 2026.8.28, with the National Assembly Research Service flagging unresolved gaps in data quality, AI reliability/accountability, and governance/security as implementation challenges. [2]
  • ETRI joined the Agentic AI Foundation under the Linux Foundation, aiming to shape agentic AI standards such as MCP from the outset, including working groups on security/privacy and governance/risk response. [3]
25 August 2026
  • Security researchers and Korean media warned of memory poisoning attacks on AI agents, where malicious data injected into long-term memory persistently distorts future decisions and actions, distinct from one-off prompt injection; the Personal Information Protection Commission plans to issue AI agent guidelines in the second half of the year. [1][2]
  • The government finalized Korea's AI Ethics Principles under the AI Basic Act, setting three core values (human dignity, public good, sustainability) and seven principles including safety, accountability, and transparency, explicitly naming users as responsible actors alongside developers; the non-binding self-regulatory code will be supported by case-based guides and self-check lists. [3][4][5][6]
  • ETRI joined the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation (AAIF), a roughly 250-member alliance including Google and OpenAI, to help set international standards such as MCP for agentic AI systems. [7]
24 August 2026
  • MSIT finalized Korea's first revamped AI ethics principles in six years, built on three core values (human dignity, social good, sustainability) and seven principles including privacy, accountability, and transparency, explicitly requiring human control over agentic AI and imposing proportional accountability on large AI providers; the code remains non-binding self-regulation. [1][2][3][4][5][6][7]
  • The same Science and ICT ministers' meeting also adopted a roadmap to expand autonomous-driving AI field tests with an accompanying safety and accident-response system, alongside a deep-tech startup funding plan. [7]
22 August 2026
  • Korea's Personal Information Protection Act was revised to allow use of personal data for AI training beyond pseudonymized/anonymized data when public-interest need is shown and Personal Information Protection Commission deliberation is passed, requiring enhanced safeguards and prior risk assessments for sensitive or unique-identifier data; the amendment takes effect six months after promulgation, converting a temporary regulatory-sandbox exemption into a permanent statutory system. [1]
21 August 2026
  • South Korea's National Assembly passed an amendment to the Personal Information Protection Act creating an exception allowing collection of personal data for AI development when pseudonymized or anonymized data is insufficient, subject to enhanced safeguards and review by the Personal Information Protection Commission, with mandatory prior risk assessment and disclosure for sensitive or unique identifier data; it takes effect six months after promulgation. [1]
20 August 2026
  • South Korea's National Assembly passed a Personal Information Protection Act amendment allowing companies to use original personal data for AI training when it serves public interest, subject to prior review by the Personal Information Protection Commission, mandatory risk assessments for high-risk data, and public disclosure of processing policies and special-case usage. [1]
19 August 2026
  • In the second-round evaluation of the Independent AI Foundation Model project (Dokpamo), Upstage, SK Telecom, and LG AI Research advanced while Motif Technologies was eliminated, with passing teams to receive roughly 1,000 B200 GPUs each ahead of a final selection of two teams in early 2027. [1][2][3][4][5]
  • Critics noted MSIT withheld detailed score breakdowns and rankings from the Dokpamo evaluation despite it being taxpayer-funded, arguing transparency is needed given public money at stake. [6]
  • MSIT is reviewing a broader restructuring of AI frontier programs, including Dokpamo, to reconsider competition rules, resource allocation, and consortium structure after the next evaluation round. [1]
18 August 2026
  • Independent AI Foundation Model second-round evaluation eliminated Motif Technologies despite its top benchmark score, citing weak usability and ecosystem-contribution ratings, while officials said a review with Artificial Analysis found no evidence of benchmark overfitting. [1][2][3]
  • A National Assembly Research Service report on the U.S. export control of Anthropic's Mythos5 model warns that high-performance AI can become a national-security strategic asset and urges Korea to strengthen sovereign AI security and governance. [4]
  • The Korea Institute of Finance called for financial firms to build AI governance covering fairness, transparency, consumer protection, and explainability for black-box models, using accountability frameworks and regulatory sandboxes. [5]
17 August 2026
  • The Ministry of Science and ICT's second draft of AI ethics principles removed non-discrimination language on gender, age, and disability, shifting bias-related responsibility onto users rather than developers, according to critics. Korean Women's Associations United demanded the draft explicitly restore diversity, non-discrimination, and inclusion principles along with clear responsibilities for developers and providers across the AI lifecycle. [1]
14 August 2026
  • MSIT and KISDI unveiled a draft revision of Korea's AI ethics principles, proposing 7 principles (including human-centeredness, safety, and fairness) built on three core values, designed as non-binding soft-law guidance rather than new regulation. [1][2]
13 August 2026
  • South Korea's proposed PIPA amendment would expand permitted uses of personal data for AI development when enhanced safety measures are in place for public-interest purposes, subject to Personal Information Protection Commission review of risk assessments and data-subject rights; the bill passed committee stages in May and July 2026 and is now under National Assembly review. [1]
12 August 2026
  • Korea's Board of Audit and Inspection found that public institutions duplicated and poorly managed AI training data built with over 1.6 trillion won in government investment, urging MSIT and other agencies to adopt shared review systems and unified quality standards. [1]
11 August 2026
  • The Korea Institute of Finance and Economics published a brief on AI shopping agents, arguing that financial regulators must prepare consumer-protection rules covering the right to information, conflict-of-interest prevention, and fair competition among agents before such agents reach retail customers. [1]
  • KISA and IITP convened an industry council, led by newly appointed PM 조학수, to identify information-security R&D priorities including AI agent security, zero trust, and homomorphic encryption for emerging tech environments. [2]
10 August 2026
  • A Korean economic research institute's briefing on frontier AI development regulation argues that verification difficulty, self-interest of states and firms, and political-economic constraints make outright bans impractical, urging instead managing development direction, building societal resilience, and securing transparency and accountability from frontier developers. [1]
9 August 2026
  • Following a forum on physical-AI malfunction risks from robotic manipulation, MSIT unveiled an 'Agentic AI Initiative' to develop safety guidelines and sandbox support for humanoid robots, while critics noted the ISO 13482 standard revision still lacks adequate treatment of human-robot interaction and value judgments. [1]
7 August 2026
  • Korea's AI Safety Institute found no domestic or foreign AI model underwent pre-release safety verification, after evaluating 42 models, unlike the U.S. system where firms submit models to government for pre-release review [1].
6 August 2026
  • MSIT and KISA published an AI security red-teaming guide, as global attention grows on red-teaming to test AI systems—including training data, core models, applications, and autonomous agent behavior—from an attacker's perspective. [1]
4 August 2026
  • MSIT released guidelines on AI data quality management, generative AI data quality management, and AI data construction. [1]
  • The Ministry of Health and Welfare issued a guideline on the use of health and medical data. [2]
  • The Personal Information Protection Commission published a guideline on processing pseudonymized information. [3]
3 August 2026
  • Korea's data protection authority (PIPC) asked Samsung and Meta to add privacy safeguards to upcoming AI glasses, including recording-notification features, ahead of finalizing personal-data protection principles for such devices this year. [1]
  • A government cybersecurity-specific AI development program is at risk of stalling due to scheduling overlap with other AI projects, with almost no firms committing to participate; MSIT said it may cancel operator selection if no suitable bidder emerges. [2]
2 August 2026
  • An op-ed criticizes Korea's autonomous vehicle law and physical AI special bill for permitting personal-data use under vague "unavoidable" exceptions that prioritize industrial competitiveness, warning of missing standards and inadequate civil-society consultation before such clauses become law. [1]
